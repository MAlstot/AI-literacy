Prompt Guide: Keep the Thinking Yours

How to ask AI for help that supports your learning instead of replacing it.

You don't need AI to study well. But when you use it, by choice or because a class requires it, you stay in charge of the thinking.

Everything here is a starting point, not a script. Copy what fits, change it to match your class, and check your syllabus for your professor's AI policy first.

The Four Parts of a Prompt

A prompt that keeps you thinking tells AI four things.

  1. Context

    What you're working on, the requirements, and where you are in it.

  2. Role

    The job you want AI to do, with a short description of that job.

  3. Help Needed

    One specific goal, so AI knows what success looks like.

  4. Rails

    The rules of engagement: what AI can and can't do, and how the conversation goes.

Context: give details

  • Who you are: "I'm a first-year student in English 1302."
  • What the assignment is: paste in the assignment directions and the grading criteria or rubric.
  • The requirements: length, format, sources, due date.
  • Where you are in it: just starting, first draft done, studying for the exam.

Help Needed: name one goal

"Help me" and "give me feedback" make AI guess. Pick one goal at a time.

  • Writing: "Tell me whether my main point is clear, and where it gets fuzzy."
  • Reading: "Ask me how and why questions about chapter 3." (encoding)
  • Exam prep: "Quiz me on the study guide topics I missed last time." (retrieval practice)
  • Practice problems: "Show me which step has the error, and help me figure out why I made it. Don't fix it for me." (worked examples)

Role Cards

A role is a title plus what that job does. The higher the role on the AI Cognitive Use Pyramid, the more of the thinking stays with you. Copy a card into the Role part of your prompt.

Two roles you won't assign. Evidence isn't a role you give AI. It's what you do when you check AI's work against what you know. Executor (AI does the task for you) fits tasks that aren't the learning, like turning due dates into a calendar.

Rails: Rules of Engagement

A role tells AI what job to do. Rails set the rules of engagement. You need both. Stack two to four rails at the end of your prompt.

    When AI Goes Off Track, Revise

    Even a good prompt doesn't always work the first time. You're in charge, so correct it. Ask which part needs fixing, then try again.

    Check every response. Did AI follow your rails, or did it do the thinking for you?

    Bring a Human Into the Loop

    Getting help in college is normal, and successful students do it often. AI can be one place to start. It can help you figure out what you're stuck on, so you walk in with better questions and get more out of the session.

    At Baylor: your professor's office hours, your TA, tutoring at the Paul L. Foster Success Center, the Center for Writing Excellence, and your subject librarian.
