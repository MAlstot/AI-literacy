In our workshop, we'll use AI fluency and literacy as lenses for isolating the thinking your students most need — so they gain the cognitive knowledge and AI competencies to be competitive in an AI-integrated workforce. This primer introduces the concepts we'll apply during the workshop. In preparation, please:

  • Watch the six short videos as an overview of the 4D framework we'll use as our shared language.
  • Come with a course (or, even better, one assignment) in mind and a clear sense of its kernel of cognition — the core thinking that, if a student handed it to AI, the course would lose its point.
  • Bring your course syllabus and/or course learning outcomes — they're the raw material we'll work from.

Developing AI literacy includes assignments that prohibit AI use. If you carry real reservations about AI on a specific assignment (or course), we'll discuss how AI-resistant assignments also contribute to developing AI fluency and literacy.

When we meet, we'll work two questions together, with your course on the table:

What's the kernel of cognition in your course or assignment?What thinking must stay the student's own for the learning to still mean something?
Imagine the useWhere could AI make your students stronger practitioners — without short-circuiting that thinking?

First · Watch the 4Ds

The AI Fluency 4D Framework (~30 min of short videos)

Our workshop runs on the AI Fluency Framework — four competencies (the "4Ds") developed by Rick Dakan, Joseph Feller, and Anthropic. Watch these six short lessons (~5 minutes each) from Anthropic's free video course. Start with the introduction and the overview, then the four Ds in any order — and as you watch, hold each video's question against the course you're bringing:

  1. Introduction to AI Fluency · ~5 minWhat "fluency" means beyond prompting — the ground the 4Ds stand on.
  2. The 4D Framework — overview · ~5 minThe map for our workshop — where in your course does each D live?
  3. A closer look at Delegation · ~5 minWhat thinking is the student's to keep — and what could AI appropriately take on?
  4. A closer look at Description · ~5 minDo your students know how to tell AI what they actually want — context, constraints, and what it should NOT do?
  5. A closer look at Discernment · 5:23Are your students equipped to judge what AI returns — against what they know, not just how it sounds?
  6. A closer look at Diligence · ~5 minDoes your course ask students to own, verify, and disclose what AI did?

Want the full picture? The complete 12-video course playlist adds lessons on how generative AI works and effective prompting.
