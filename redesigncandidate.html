Now the honest part

Your assignment will not be as clean as the assignments in the practice activity.

Five assignments, five classifications, and every one of them arrived with its learning objective stated in a tidy italic line and its cognitive tasks already separated for you. That is not what an assignment looks like. That is what an assignment looks like after someone has already done the analysis — and there, that someone was us.

What you are about to open is your own assignment from Module 3, and it will be messy in the specific ways real assignments are messy:

The stated objective will not match the real one. The syllabus says "students will analyze"; the rubric awards most of its points for formatting and length. Both are true, and they point in opposite directions. You will have to decide which one the assignment is actually built around.

The tasks will not come pre-separated. What reads as one step — "prepare a market entry recommendation" — is four cognitive tasks wearing a trench coat, and some of them are protected while others are wide open. Nobody has pulled them apart. You will.

And you will be wrong about some of it — not because you reasoned badly, but because you are reasoning from the outside. You are guessing what AI would do to this assignment. You have not watched it happen.

That is the entire point of what comes next. The reflection below asks for your first pass, made on instinct, from the outside, with the lens you just practiced. It is supposed to be provisional. Then on Day 2, you run the assignment through an AI Stress Test and stop guessing — you watch the tool do the work, side by side with your expectations, and the split you drew here moves. The Gap Analysis is where instinct becomes evidence, task by task.

So write the messy version. Name the task you cannot place. The uncertainty you carry into Day 2 is not a gap in your preparation — it is the material Day 2 is made of.

The continuum, applied — Module 4 returns

Your assignment is not at a level. Its tasks are at different levels.

In Module 4 you placed this assignment on the IWU AI Literacy Continuum and gave it a number. That number was useful, and it was also a simplification — because you had not yet pulled the assignment apart. Now you have.

So here is the harder question. Look at your two columns above and place them separately. Where do the tasks you want to protect sit on the continuum? And where do the tasks you want to open sit — not where they are today, but where the redesign would put them?

Most faculty find these two numbers are not the same. That gap is not an error in your thinking. It is the shape of the redesign. An assignment that protects at Level 1 and opens at Level 4 is not confused — it is deliberately doing two things, and it is the most honest kind of assignment you can build in a discipline where students will graduate into both realities.

If your two numbers come out identical, that is a real finding too — it usually means one column is thin, and it is worth going back up to look again.

You are not being asked to resolve this. You are being asked to see it clearly enough to bring it to Day 2.
